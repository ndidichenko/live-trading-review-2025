# 05. What this is not

## Selection

This repo documents **2025-03-09 to 2025-12-30**, the first year I logged
properly. Trading continued into 2026, but it is not in scope here, ask and I
will give you the number and what I did about it.

- **2021-2023** inconsistent trading, gambling basically.
- **2024** learning, researching and backtesting, while holding BTC.
- **2025** +24.71R, +40%, max drawdown -29.0%. The documented year.
- **2026** ongoing.

The reason 2025 is the documented year is that it is the first one with a record
good enough to document. That is also the honest limitation. A single profitable
year selected from a career that contains unprofitable ones is a sample of one,
and the write-up is worth what the process is worth. Positive return is just a nice-to-have outcome of the properly executed and consistently followed process.

## Not transferable as-is

**What carries over.** 
- Sizing from risk rather than conviction, with 1R defined
before entry and position size derived from stop distance. 
- Running a live test at
a size where being wrong is affordable, and only scaling on evidence. 
- Knowing the
shape of your own return distribution and sizing for it rather than being
surprised by it. 
- Reconciling two independent records and treating the
disagreements as findings, which is how the timezone defect and the spreadsheet
date defect in this repo were caught.
- Keeping a written record good enough that
someone else can audit the decisions.

**What does not.** 
- This is a directional momentum book,
and that is close to the opposite of market making. I have never quoted two-sided,
managed inventory against adverse selection, or earned a spread. The skill of
being filled on the wrong side and pricing that risk is not demonstrated anywhere
in this record.
- I have no live options or Greeks exposure. The horizon is minutes
to days rather than microseconds, and execution was manual. 
- One instrument, one
venue, one regime. Most importantly the statistical profile is inverted. A market
maker wins often and small and relies on the law of large numbers, while this book
loses 77% of the time and relies on a handful of tail events, so almost none of my
intuition about sample size and variance transfers directly.

The honest summary is that this record is evidence of risk discipline, research
process and the ability to sit with a losing streak, and it is not evidence of
market-making ability. Those would have to be assessed separately.
