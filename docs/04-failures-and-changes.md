# 04. Decisions

Six decisions, not six outcomes, they differ. A decision is judgeable on the
information available when it was made, and an outcome is not.

---

## Three I would still make

### 1. Size up gradually, and only after a loss and a winner at the current size

Sizing up gradually, from $1 to $2 to $4, and not moving up until I had at least
one loss and one winner at the previous size. I am glad I stuck to $1 to $2 to
$4 and did not jump from $1 to $10 to $100. Even when it looks like a small
increase, only $1 or $3, it is relatively a big jump, it is a 2x increase. It helped me
manage my emotions, handle a loss, handle losing big unrealised profits, stay
disciplined about the process and care less about the money. That is the decision
I recommend.

**What the log shows.** The ramp ran 2025-03-09 to 2025-05-31: 81 trades, +4.40R,
+998 USD, with risk per trade stepping

```
2.2 -> 3.5 -> 4 -> 5.7 -> 8 -> 10 -> 15 -> 20 -> 40 -> 50 -> 75
```

Eleven sizes in under three months, no step above 2x, and the book was profitable
the whole way through at money small enough that being wrong cost nothing.

### 2. Trade one fixed window and be fully present in it

Having a fixed trading window where I focus most. Crypto trades 24/7 and I cannot
sit on the charts 24/7, so pick the time that suits you best, when you can be
100% focused on the charts, the systems and the trading. That is when you get the
most out of it. For me it was the NY session, 9:30am to 4:00pm ET.

### 3. Research, backtest, review and journal outside the trading window

Continue researching, backtesting new systems, reviewing and journaling trades
outside your trading hours.

## Three I would not

### 1. Adding new strategies at full risk instead of scaling each one separately

Newer strategies entered my book at my current risk rather than at a reduced one.
I think that was an impatient decision. I should have scaled each strategy
separately, the way I scaled myself, to see whether its edge persisted.

**What it cost.** Sleeve G ran 49 trades from 2025-04-15 to 2025-12-04 for
**-18.30R**, against a full-year book total of +24.71R.

### 2. Keeping a sleeve that was already losing

Sleeve G was **-6.65R after its first 24 trades**. It stayed in the book and lost
a further **-11.65R over its next 25**. The information needed to cut it existed
halfway through, and no rule required a decision at that point.

### 3. Having no calendar risk limits

2025-11-01 to 2025-12-30: 104 trades, **-20.91R**, -6,065 USD, at full 275 USD
risk throughout. That is more trades in two losing months than in October, the
best month of the year. A daily, weekly or monthly loss limit could have softened
this drawdown.

---

## Changes already identified

- Calendar risk limits: 2R a day, 5R a week, 10R a month
- Scale each new sleeve separately before it joins the book at full risk
- Cut a sleeve on a rule, not on a feeling

What I would measure to know if it works:
- For calendar risk limits - does it decrease max draw down? Compare max and mean DD With and without it.
- Quality (live CAGR and max and mean DD) of the strategies that enter live book.
